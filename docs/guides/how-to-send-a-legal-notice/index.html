Getting started

A legal notice is a formal letter that sets out your grievance, says what you want, and gives the other side time to fix it before you go to court. It often resolves a dispute on its own, and it creates a written record that you tried to settle.

Is a legal notice compulsory?

For most private disputes it is not compulsory, but it is a good practice. In some situations the law requires a notice before you can act:

  • Before suing the government or a public officer for an official act, two months' notice is required (Section 80, Code of Civil Procedure, 1908).
  • For a bounced cheque, a written demand must be sent within 30 days of learning that the cheque was dishonoured (Section 138, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881).
  • To end a monthly tenancy, notice must be given as required by the agreement or Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882.

What to include

  • Full names and addresses of both sides
  • The facts in order, with dates and amounts
  • The documents you rely on (agreement, receipts, messages)
  • Exactly what you want: payment, refund, repair, or to stop doing something
  • A clear deadline, often 15 to 30 days
  • What you will do if they don't comply

How to send it

Send it by registered post with acknowledgement due or by speed post, and keep the receipt and tracking printout. Sending a copy by email or messaging app as well helps prove delivery. A lawyer's letterhead is not required, but it can add weight.

“ Keep a copy of everything you send and receive. In most disputes, the side with better records is in the stronger position. ”

After you send it

If they reply, read the reply carefully: it may be an opening for settlement. If they ignore it, the next step depends on the dispute, for example a consumer commission, a civil court, or a Magistrate in a cheque case. Constitution Ai can draft a notice for you to review.
