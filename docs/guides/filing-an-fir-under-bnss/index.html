Police & criminal

Since 1 July 2024, a First Information Report (FIR) is registered under Section 173 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), which replaced Section 154 of the Code of Criminal Procedure. An FIR starts a police investigation into a cognizable offence, such as theft, assault or cheating.

How to file

  • Go to the police station and give the information orally or in writing. If oral, it is written down and read back to you before you sign.
  • Zero FIR: you can file at any police station, even if the crime happened elsewhere. It is then transferred to the right station.
  • e-FIR: information can be given electronically, and it is taken on record once you sign it within three days.
  • You are entitled to a free copy of the FIR.

If the police refuse

Send the substance of your complaint in writing, by post, to the Superintendent of Police. If that does not work, you can apply to the Magistrate under Section 175(3) BNSS (earlier Section 156(3) CrPC) asking for the police to be directed to investigate.

“ For offences punishable with three to seven years, the police may first conduct a preliminary enquiry, which should be completed within 14 days. ”

Non-cognizable offences

For minor, non-cognizable offences, the police record the information in a register and refer you to the Magistrate; they cannot investigate without the Magistrate's order.

In an emergency, call 112. If you are accused of an offence or arrested, contact a criminal lawyer or your District Legal Services Authority for free legal aid.
